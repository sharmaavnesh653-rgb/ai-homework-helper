For teachers

Built to survive your scrutiny.

Most AI homework tools are banned in classrooms for good reason: they are answer vending machines with a policy page bolted on. We would rather show you exactly how this one behaves and let you decide.

Six commitments

These are properties of the product, not promises in a footer.

01

Hint mode cannot be talked into the answer

The instruction is explicit: give one nudge, never the solution, never the remaining steps — including at the end of the reply. A student who wants the answer has to reason their way to it.

02

We never rewrite a student’s work

“Check my work” names what’s right, finds the first place the reasoning breaks, and explains the misconception behind it. It is instructed not to produce a corrected version. The improved draft has to come from them.

03

We will never ship an “AI humanizer”

Several tools in this category sell a detector and a humanizer side by side — one product to flag AI text, another to disguise it. That is a business model built on defeating you. We won’t build it at any price.

04

Every reply names its method

Steps are tied to the actual rule or concept in play — the distributive property, a thesis statement, conservation of momentum — so students build vocabulary they can carry into class, not just a number to write down.

05

It admits when it isn’t sure

The tutor is instructed to flag uncertainty rather than assert a confident wrong answer, and never to confirm work as correct without checking it. Students are told plainly to verify before submitting.

06

Transcripts are designed to be read by you

A session looks like a tutoring record: the question, the reasoning, the check question left unanswered. Nothing is structured to be hidden from a teacher, because nothing needs to be.

What it can’t do

You will not find this section on a competitor's site. It is the fastest way to tell whether a tool is being straight with you.

  • — It is a language model. It can be confidently wrong, especially on multi-step arithmetic and anything requiring a source it hasn’t been given.
  • — It cannot see your assignment brief, rubric, or what you covered in class unless the student pastes it in.
  • — A determined student can still use walkthrough mode and copy the reasoning. Hint mode raises the effort of doing that; it does not make it impossible.
  • — We are not a substitute for you. It is a patient explainer for the moment a student is stuck at 9pm with nobody to ask.

Try to break it

The most useful thing you can do is take a question from your own homework set, put it in hint mode, and push it for the answer. Then put a flawed student paragraph into check mode and see whether it rewrites it. That will tell you more than any page we could write.
